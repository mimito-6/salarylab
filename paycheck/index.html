The math behind it

regular = hourly × regular_hours
overtime = hourly × multiplier × overtime_hours
gross per paycheck = regular + overtime
estimated annual = gross per paycheck × paychecks per year

Example: $25/hr × 80 regular hours = $2,000, plus 10 overtime hours at 1.5× = $375, gives $2,375 gross per biweekly paycheck — $61,750 over 26 paychecks, at an effective $26.39/hr across all 90 hours. Watch the semimonthly trap: at a 40-hour week a semimonthly period averages 86.67 hours (2,080 ÷ 24), not 80 — entering 80 understates the year by $4,800 at $30/hr. All figures are gross, before taxes and deductions, and the annual figure assumes every paycheck repeats this one.

Frequently asked questions

How is my gross paycheck calculated?

Regular pay is your hourly wage times your regular hours in the pay period; overtime pay is your wage times your overtime multiplier times your overtime hours. Add them together. Example: $25/hr × 80 regular hours = $2,000, plus 10 overtime hours at 1.5× ($375), gives $2,375 gross per biweekly paycheck.

Why is my biweekly paycheck not half of my monthly pay?

Because biweekly pay has 26 paychecks a year and monthly pay has 12. A $2,375 biweekly check annualizes to $61,750, which is $5,145.83 a month — not $4,750. Biweekly pay also gives you two months a year with three paychecks.

How many hours go in a semimonthly pay period?

At a 40-hour week, a semimonthly period averages 86.67 hours (2,080 hours a year ÷ 24 checks), not 80. Entering 80 hours for a semimonthly check understates your year: $30/hr × 80 hours × 24 checks = $57,600, while the correct 86.67-hour figure gives $62,400.

Are these figures gross or net of taxes?

Gross. All figures are before income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and any benefits deductions. Your take-home pay will be lower, and the exact difference depends on your tax situation.

What does the annual estimate assume?

It assumes every paycheck in the year looks exactly like the one you entered — same hours, same overtime, same rate. If your overtime varies, the annual figure is a projection of this paycheck, not a promise.

Does this apply to salaried or exempt workers?

Not necessarily. This calculator assumes you are paid by the hour with an overtime premium. Exempt salaried workers often do not earn overtime pay at all, and eligibility depends on your job classification and local law. The tool calculates the math; it does not give legal advice.
