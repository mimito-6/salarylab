The math behind it

commute_hours = daily_hours × days_per_week × 52
money_cost = monthly_transport × 12 + extra_yearly_cost
time_cost = commute_hours × your_hourly_wage (optional)

Fixed costs such as car payments and insurance are not included — you owe them whether or not you commute. The time value is an opportunity-cost estimate: what those hours would be worth at your wage, not money you would actually have earned.

Frequently asked questions

How much does commuting really cost per year?

It costs money plus time. Add your yearly transit or fuel expenses to the value of your commute hours. For example, a 1-hour daily round trip, 5 days a week, is 260 hours a year; valued at a $48.08/hour wage that is $12,500 in time alone.

Should I count commute time as lost income?

That is up to you. Valuing commute hours at your hourly wage shows the opportunity cost — what that time would be worth at your rate of pay. It is an estimate of lost value, not money you would have earned.

Is a longer commute ever worth it for higher pay?

Compare the salary difference against your commute cost. If the raise is smaller than what the commute costs you in time and money each year, the higher-paying job pays you less in real terms.

How do I estimate my fuel and toll costs?

Start with your monthly spend: fuel, tolls, parking, and transit passes. Multiply by 12, then add any yearly costs like vehicle maintenance tied to commuting. Enter the monthly figure and extra yearly costs in the calculator.

Does the calculator include car payments or insurance?

No. Fixed costs like car payments and insurance are due whether or not you commute, so they are excluded. Only include costs that scale with commuting, plus the optional time value of your commute hours.

How does remote work compare?

Working from home removes both the money cost and the time cost of commuting. Enter your current commute details to see the total yearly value a remote or hybrid schedule would give you back.
